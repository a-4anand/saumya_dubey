BMR and BPR are often mentioned together because both are central to batch documentation, but they do not serve exactly the same purpose.

## BMR

BMR generally focuses on manufacturing activity. It captures the instructions and recorded details related to production steps, material usage, process flow and in-process control points within the agreed documentation framework.

## BPR

BPR generally focuses on packaging activity. It covers packaging instructions, coding, reconciliation and the packaging-side record trail for the batch.

## Why the distinction matters

When the two are treated as interchangeable, review quality often drops. Manufacturing issues may be reviewed too lightly, or packaging controls may not receive enough attention.

## A practical review mindset

During review, ask:

- Is the record complete?
- Is the version current?
- Are corrections handled properly?
- Do the entries align with the expected sequence of work?
- Are there inconsistencies that need clarification before final approval?

The exact structure depends on the company system, but the underlying principle is the same: the record should support traceability, clarity and disciplined review.
