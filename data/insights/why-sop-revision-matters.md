An SOP can be approved and still become less useful over time. Operations change, responsibilities shift and forms evolve. When the document does not keep pace, the gap shows up in training, execution and review.

## Revision protects usability

An outdated SOP can create confusion even when everyone is trying to do the right thing. Staff may rely on verbal workarounds, informal habits or parallel instructions that never made it into the controlled document.

## Revision protects consistency

If one team follows the old format and another team follows a newer expectation, the quality system starts to feel fragmented. Revision helps restore a single source of truth.

## Revision supports audit readiness

Auditors and internal reviewers often look for whether documents are controlled, current and actually reflective of practice. A revision process does not need to be dramatic, but it does need to be disciplined.

## Practical questions to ask

- Is the SOP still aligned with current practice?
- Have linked forms, logs or references changed?
- Is the revision backlog visible and prioritized?
- Are users likely to understand the document without extra interpretation?

Revision is not just an administrative task. It is part of how a quality system stays coherent over time.
